## 论文与模型

1. **[StreamVibe: Proactive Video Agents for Evolving User Intent](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34662)**

   Vision-language models are shifting from offline passive understanding toward online proactive interaction. However, prevailing methods largely rely on explicit requests while miss the implicit user intent (e.g., "where should I click next?" while a user navigate a website), meanwhile fail to update the state simultaneously as user requests might update continuously. These limitations make them less suited to intent-aware interaction in practical. To address these challenges, we introduce StreamVibe-Bench, a new benchmark serving as testbed for user intent interaction, with 4.1K evaluation samples and 85K training samples spanning explicit, implicit, and dynamic intents. Building on this benchmark, we develop StreamVibe-VL, a streaming proactive model that infers implicit user intents from continuous observations and dynamically tailors its interactions as these intents evolve. We further introduce StreamVibe-Harness, which maintains user intents as persistent states with a fleixble lifecycles via a hierarchical memory to track the latest user intent and environmental state. Our experiments reveal three key findings: (i) StreamVibe-VL preserves strong general video understanding while improving response timing and answer quality by 10.7% over other baselines; (ii) existing proactive models remain limited in understanding user intent and deciding when to respond, especially under implicit and dynamic intents; and (iii) StreamVibe-Harness further improves response consistency and adaptation over sustained interaction, resulting in a 12.3% gain in proactive interaction.

   来源：HuggingFace Papers

2. **[Work Agents: Systems, Capabilities, and Optimization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35845)**

   As foundation models evolve from information generation to digital work execution, agents are increasingly capable of completing complex tasks across tools, applications, and workflows. However, existing research lacks a unified perspective on how agents reliably accomplish delegated work. To address this gap, we present a work-centric, system-level perspective that views work agents as systems combining foundation models and runtime harnesses to execute tasks in stateful workspaces and deliver verifiable outcomes. We introduce two complementary dimensions, work persistence and work openness, to characterize working conditions, and classify digital work into five forms according to task scope and organization. Building on this framework, we identify five core capability families required for reliable work execution and examine approaches to model optimization, harness optimization, and their joint optimization. We further analyze existing evaluation methods and metrics from the perspective of work outcomes. Finally, we discuss future research directions, including sustained work delegation, adaptation to open environments, and the interaction between work execution and capability improvement. This paper provides a unified framework for understanding, optimizing, and evaluating work agents, supporting the development of systems capable of delivering reliable, verifiable, and practically valuable work outcomes.

   来源：HuggingFace Papers

3. **[Trustworthy Recursive Self-Improvement in the Era of Large Models: Foundation, Risks, Evaluation and Open Challenges](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35193)**

   AI systems built around large models increasingly use experience and feedback to make persistent changes to models, prompts, memories, tools, and workflows. Recursive self-improvement (RSI) extends this process to inductive biases and to the mechanisms that govern future updates, which can themselves be revised and reused across improvement cycles. Yet self-modification alone does not guarantee trustworthy improvement. Reported gains may reflect flawed evaluations, previously acquired capabilities may deteriorate, and compliance with behavioral constraints may weaken. Compromised artifacts or sensitive information may also persist in successor systems. Changes to improvement mechanisms may invalidate earlier verification results and make human oversight less effective. This survey presents an operational definition and process framework for RSI and organizes the requirements for trustworthy RSI into four interconnected themes: genuine and sustained improvement; behavioral constraints, alignment, and fairness; system security and privacy; and transparency and controllability. We examine these themes from three perspectives: Improvement Validity, Constraint Preservation, and Effective Oversight. This analysis covers improvement diagnosis, proposal generation, proposal evaluation, and update implementation (S1--S4), including the recursive application of these stages to improvement mechanisms (RI). We review risks, safeguards, and evaluation evidence for individual updates, effects inherited by successor systems, and revisions to improvement mechanisms, while distinguishing direct RSI findings from evidence in related systems. Finally, we examine evaluation settings, identify requirements for independent and longitudinal assessment, and discuss applications and open research problems.

   来源：HuggingFace Papers

4. **[S3AC: Stabilized, Selective, and Scalable Actor-Critic for LLM Reasoning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35518)**

   Critic-free methods are widely used in reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) for long chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning, avoiding difficult and costly critic training. Common alternatives lack token-level credit assignment and typically rely on grouped rollouts to estimate prompt-specific baselines. A critic can address both limitations, but long-CoT critic training is hindered by insufficient coverage of trajectories with diverse lengths and early gradients dominated by the newly initialized value head. We introduce S3AC (Stabilized, Selective, and Scalable Actor-Critic) for LLM reasoning, built around a practical critic-training recipe. The trained critic provides token-specific advantages and prompt-specific baselines. Using the prompt-level baseline to estimate difficulty, we propose Difficulty-Aware Prompt Sampling (DAPS). DAPS combines this estimate with outcome history to select prompts before generation, reducing rollout waste without offline difficulty initialization. S3AC improves group-rollout performance across dense and mixture-of-experts models from 1.5B to 397B parameters, outperforming a modern GRPO variant (GRPO-X). At 35B, it sustains single-rollout training while tested critic-free baselines deteriorate after initial gains.

   来源：HuggingFace Papers

5. **[EmbodiedLab: Benchmarking Embodied Agents That Act to Know](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35385)**

   Embodied agents are expected to accomplish real-world goals by deciding for themselves how and when to interact with their surroundings. For many such goals, changing the physical state of the world is the means rather than the end: the agent changes the state of a system through interaction, observes the feedback, and explores until it either achieves the goal itself (e.g., discovering physical laws) or learns what the goal requires; it acts to know. Manipulation benchmarks stop at acting to change, with the target state given; scientific discovery benchmarks evaluate knowing but replace physical experimentation with symbolic actions or numerical queries. We introduce EmbodiedLab, a benchmark in which a robot must act to know. Quantitative science experiments make this setting controllable: the parameters of the system are resampled for every instance, so the result cannot be known in advance, yet it can be scored exactly. Across 30 experiments in five disciplines on 14 apparatus platforms, the agent receives an objective and the apparatus, chooses its own procedure, and operates a physics-based simulated laboratory, reading the instruments through its cameras. Evaluation checks what the agent came to know, grounded in readings recorded during the episode, as well as the physical state the task requires; every experiment is verified to be solvable. We evaluate six frontier models in their developers' coding agents with native observation and control, with added perception and motion planning, and with manipulation skills distilled from the strongest agents. With native control, even the strongest agent succeeds less than half the time; for every agent, most failures arise in interacting with the apparatus, and manipulation skills help weaker agents more than perception and planning. The benchmark and its evaluation code are available at https://github.com/HexaFuture/EmbodiedLab.

   来源：HuggingFace Papers

6. **[More Memory, Better Videos? A Controlled Study of Visual Memory in Long-Video Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35001)**

   Long-video generators increasingly retrieve past visual observations to prevent identity drift. The intuition is compelling: more access to history should make future video more consistent. But an image can be faithful to the past and wrong for the present. Does visual memory always improve the generated story? We investigate this question through VMem-Bench, a two-track diagnostic study. Track-A measures which references are selected from 92 real films (16,548 segments), before generation and without generator confounds. Track-B compares four retrieval policies with no-memory and gold-informed last-seen controls on 30 causally generated stories (2,824 segments) using a shared generator. Retrieval increases entity recall from 0.559 to 0.628-0.693 and improves similarity to past appearances, but decreases precision from 0.977 to 0.915-0.938 and forbidden-entity avoidance from 0.968 to 0.845-0.898. It can also reinforce obsolete appearances after the story changes them. Conversely, high long-gap recall can arise without memory when the prompt itself supplies the appearance; an appearance-stripping analysis exposes this confound. These results distinguish historical fidelity from current-story correctness and show why retrieval relevance or final-video consistency alone does not establish memory utility.

7. **[Thinking-in-Bets at Test Time Reduces Sycophantic Revisions in LLM Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34654)**

   Language model agents often abandon correct work when a user challenges it, yet simply telling them to stand firm makes them ignore real mistakes. Once an agent reads a complaint, it tends to discount its own earlier checks in order to agree with the user. We introduce Thinking-in-Bets, a training-free method in which an agent writes a short private record of its main claims, the checks supporting them, and its confidence before reading user feedback. When challenged, the agent consults this Claim Checklist to keep verified work intact while investigating uncertain or newly challenged parts. We evaluate five Gemini models (3.5 Flash-Lite, 3.5 Flash, 3.6 Flash, 3.8 Flash, and 4.0 Argon) across eight benchmarks: SWE-Together, SWE-Interact, and DeepSWE for software engineering, Agents' Last Exam for professional reports, Humanity's Last Exam and SycEval for academic reasoning, Vals Finance for financial analysis, and Terminal-Bench for command-line tasks. Recording claims before criticism nearly eliminates false reversals in patch review and protects passing report drafts under mistaken pushback, whereas writing the same record after reading the complaint recovers very little of the gain. In multi-turn coding, pre-reply claim records substantially reduce unnecessary file edits after mistaken complaints, and gating the record so it is written only when the agent is uncertain lowers token overhead while fixing more real bugs than the baseline. Even in non-interactive tasks without user feedback, listing the key claims in a draft and re-checking the weakest one before submission improves both answer accuracy and confidence estimates across all tested models.

8. **[Skill^{infty}: Recursive Self-Improvement for Agent Skill Optimization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35434)**

   Recursive self-improvement (RSI), in which a system also improves the way it improves, is a long-standing aspiration on the path to AGI. Language agents increasingly store procedural knowledge in reusable skills. Optimizing skills offers a direct way to achieve RSI, because the optimization procedure itself can be encoded as a skill. Existing skill optimizers, however, follow a hand-designed procedure. Even methods that evolve a meta-skill confine it to a fixed pipeline and relearn it for each task. We present Skill^{infty}, which makes skill optimization recursively self-improving: a meta agent follows a meta-skill to optimize task skills and optimizes the meta-skill itself using the same procedure. The optimization is agent-led. The meta agent diagnoses execution traces, proposes candidates, requests independent reviews, and allocates the search budget. The meta-skill defines these steps, allowing self-improvement to rewrite them. This meta-skill is also decoupled from task skills, refined on multiple skill optimization tasks and then reused to optimize task skills across diverse domains. As in deployment, skills at both levels are standard packages loaded through native skill discovery. On 83 SkillsBench tasks, Skill^{infty} reaches a pass rate of 60.7%, compared with 56.4% for human-curated skills and 54.5% for SkillEvolver.

9. **[OBB-PBD: Extending Parallel Box Decoding to Oriented Bounding Boxes for Referring Expression Grounding in Aerial Imagery](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35450)**

   Referring expression grounding in overhead imagery requires oriented bounding boxes rather than horizontal ones, since aerial objects appear at arbitrary orientations. Most grounding models emit box coordinates sequentially; Parallel Box Decoding (PBD) instead predicts all geometry slots in one forward pass, but only for four-coordinate horizontal boxes. We extend this block-aligned interface to a six-slot oriented representation using a double-angle parameterization. Rather than assuming correctness from a decreasing loss curve, we verify the extension with image/prompt-shuffle controls and visibility/leakage probes, which show that the corrected model depends on both the image and the prompt rather than on prompt statistics alone. On the aerial vehicle visual grounding (AVVG) benchmark, whose targets are genuinely oriented, the extended model improves rotated intersection over union (rotIoU) from 0.1253 for the zero-shot baseline to 0.303 +/- 0.012 (mean +/- standard deviation over three training seeds) across the complete 6139-record test split spanning all 120 images. Learning oriented boxes rather than their axis-aligned envelopes adds 0.055 +/- 0.008 rotIoU beyond a matched control (per-seed 95% confidence intervals all excluding zero), whereas block expansion alone yields no rotIoU gain distinguishable from zero.

10. **[Constrained Decoding: Harmful or Beneficial? Revisiting Its Impact on LLM Performance](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35328)**

   Constrained decoding guarantees that Large Language Models (LLMs) produce outputs conforming to predefined formats, but is often assumed to inherently degrade reasoning. We examine this assumption through a taxonomy of three constraint types: format constraints, which enforce output structure; validity constraints, which mask invalid answer; and reasoning constraints, which restrict reasoning trajectories. We theoretically show that appropriate constraints can preserve or improve performance by eliminating invalid paths. Across four model families and eleven open-source models, causal ablations on diverse benchmarks using state-of-the-art constrained decoding strategies reveal that apparent performance degradation largely stems from non-equivalent prompting and output formats rather than from decoder-level masking. Meanwhile, validity constraints improve reliability and extractability by eliminating invalid answers, while reasoning constraints can improve reasoning and, consequently, accuracy when they enforce beneficial trajectories. These findings show that the impact of constrained decoding depends on the type of constraint and the output space it removes. Our code is available.
