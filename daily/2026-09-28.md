1. **[2026 in LLMs (so far)](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Sep/27/2026-in-llms-so-far/)**

   On Friday I gave the closing keynote at the WeAreDevelopers World Congress North America in San Jose. I tied together the key trends from the past year into a chronological exploration of everything that happened in 2026. The video is on YouTube ; here are my annotated slides and notes to accompany the talk. [图片: 2026 in LLMs (so far) Simon Willison WeAreDevelopers World Congress North America, 25th September 2026 https://static.simonwillison.net/static/2026/2026-in-llms/simon-willison-2026-in-llms-png.001.webp] # I'm going to give a lightning tour of everything that has happened so far in 2026. The year isn't over yet! [图片: November 2025 https://static.simonwillison.net/static/2026/2026-in-llms/simon-willison-2026-in-llms-png.002.webp] # For me, 2026 started a couple of months earlier in November 2025. [图片: The November 2025 inflection point Claude Opus 4.5 GPT-5.1 https://static.simonwillison.net/static/2026/2026-in-llms/simon-willison-2026-in-llms-png.003.webp] # November saw the release of two important models: Claude Opus 4.5 and GPT-5.1. As is usually the case with new models, these were incremental improvements on the models that came before them. But every now and then when a model improves, it crosses an invisible line where something that didn't really work starts working. In this case, the thing that started working was their coding agents. Claude Code had been around since February 2025, Codex was a little younger. These two new models, when paired with their respective coding agent harnesses, improved from "often make mistakes" to "reliable enough to use on a day-to-day basis". It's probably the world's stupidest benchmark - there's only so much you can learn from it.
