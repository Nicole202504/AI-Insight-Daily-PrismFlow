## 论文与模型

1. **[Scaling Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning for Brain Encoding](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26802)**

   Brain encoding models aim to predict neural responses to stimuli. The most widely used approach fits readouts from frozen pretrained backbones. Using neural data to train large models from scratch or fully fine-tune them after task-pretraining has proven difficult because neural data is costly and limited. We show that parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) enables us to effectively adapt very large pretrained backbones for brain encoding with a small number of trainable parameters. To make it computationally practical, we exploit the backbone's causal structure to predict multiple timepoints in a single pass instead of using sliding windows, significantly reducing feature-extraction cost. This enables efficient fine-tuning of multimodal backbones with up to 104B parameters, the largest model adapted for brain encoding to our knowledge. Trained with a low-rank adapter (LoRA) and a lightweight readout, our approach consistently improves brain encoding performance across nine diverse multimodal backbones. On the same Qwen3-Omni backbone as the prior state-of-the-art (SoTA), our method BrainLoRA achieves the best published performance on the Algonauts 2025 Challenge. We also show the adapted multimodal backbones remain interpretable: modality attribution recovers the canonical sensory topography that prior work often obtains by construction from separate unimodal encoders, and neural adaptation increases the contribution of audio inputs and cross-modal mixing within the backbone. To scale out the number of adapted models, we develop a multi-LoRA kernel that accelerates joint training of multiple LoRAs on a shared backbone. This enables an ensemble of 200+ adapted models that ranks first on the public Algonauts post-challenge leaderboard at the time of writing. Together, our results highlight PEFT as an efficient and powerful paradigm for brain encoding.

   来源：HuggingFace Papers

2. **[Learning from Fleet Experience: A Collective Learning Paradigm for Robots](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25736)**

   Experience generated during deployment offers an opportunity for robots to improve policy reliability through learning from their own executions. However, learning through human corrections requires teleoperators to monitor execution and intervene when policies fail, a demand that is difficult to meet as robot fleets grow. We introduce collective experience learning (CEL), a learning paradigm in which deployed robots share their execution experience to improve a common policy. We realize CEL through failure-guided experience retrieval fine-tuning (FERT), which uses failure contexts from robot executions to retrieve relevant trajectory segments from the fleet's shared successful experience and uses their actions as supervision for policy updates, without collecting additional expert demonstrations or corrective actions. A fault-tolerant asynchronous training system (FATS) coordinates experience collection, policy optimization and policy distribution despite local interruptions within the robot fleet. Across five real-world manipulation tasks, the resulting policies achieve success rates of 93.3-99.2%, evaluated over 120 trials per task on robots excluded from training data collection. With collection time per robot held fixed, larger fleets provide more experience and FERT generally achieves higher success rates across the evaluated sizes. On multi-stage phone packaging, learning with 96 robots increases success from 79.2% to 98.3%. These results support collective experience as a practical basis for scalable robot learning and a path towards improving policy reliability during deployment without collecting additional expert corrective actions.

   来源：HuggingFace Papers

3. **[MemT-1.0: Towards Memory-Native Evolution](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26825)**

   Memory-native evolution requires making memory an intrinsic capability of foundation models, enabling them to continuously accumulate and utilize information. To this end, we introduce MemT-1.0, a new architecture of memory foundation models that internalize memory as part of model computation. Building on Metis, MemT-1.0 employs Gated DeltaNet-2 with a stable token-level memory update mechanism within each turn. We further introduce Mixture-of-Storage to scale native memory capacity. Following a deliberate backbone preparation, we develop a two-stage training pipeline consisting of continued pre-training and post-training, supported by 34B tokens of training data and efficient training and inference infrastructure. We release a model family comprising MemT-1.0-Nano and MemT-1.0-Flash, and conduct a comprehensive evaluation of memory and general capabilities. The results show that they outperform the parametric memory baselines on average and remain competitive with retrieval-augmented methods, while largely preserving general capabilities. At a history length of 128K tokens, MemT-1.0-Nano achieves approximately 1.86times end-to-end inference speedup and a 66times reduction in persistent storage relative to full context KV cache. By internalizing retrieved procedural experience, MemT-1.0 improves task success on agentic tasks, providing a practical step toward memory-native evolution.

   来源：HuggingFace Papers

4. **[Holi-World: Building Holistic World Intelligence from Human-Ego Experience](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26549)**

   Human egocentric experience offers a rich foundation for learning how the world is organized and how it changes through interaction. Realizing this potential at scale requires turning growing volumes of recordings into learning resources that connect scene structure, object semantics, and interaction histories across viewpoints and time, while reducing dependence on costly manual annotation. We introduce HOLI-WORLD, an integrated ecosystem connecting experience capture, world representation, automated data construction, model learning, and independent evaluation. To sup- port learning at scale, HOLIWORLD-Foundry combines complementary static and dynamic perception to automatically construct world records that connect persistent scene structure and evolving object states with interaction histories and supporting observations. 9. **[AnyGoal: On World Model Generalization Beyond Expert Rollouts](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26844)**

Despite this, the relationship between data coverage, dynamics generalization, and downstream planning performance remains underexplored. We study this relationship through the lens of compositional generalization: can a world model recombine familiar state and action factors into valid dynamics? By examining dynamics and representation failures across model families, interaction regimes, and evaluation metrics, we study where current world models generalize and where challenges remain for goal-shifted prediction and planning. Together, this framework, benchmark, and analysis provide a basis for evaluating progress toward world models that generalize to new planning tasks.
